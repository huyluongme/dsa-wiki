# DSA Wiki

A wiki for learning and quick reference on data structures and algorithms.

Use it in two ways:

- **Learn** – read the pages in order, from the basics to more advanced topics.
- **Look up** – press the search box and jump straight to the topic you forgot.

## How the pages are organized

Every topic follows the same layout, so you always know where to look:

1. **Idea** – what it is and why it exists, with an everyday picture.
2. **How it works** – the steps, usually with a diagram.
3. **Code** – implementations you can switch between languages (C, C++, Python, Java).
4. **Complexity** – how the cost grows, in plain words.
5. **When to use it** – typical uses, and when to pick something else.
6. **Common mistakes** – what beginners often get wrong.
7. **Sources** – where the facts come from, so you can check them.

## Where to start

The data structures, in an order where each one builds on the ones before it:

1. [Array](./data-structures/array.mdx) – items side by side, with a jump straight to any of them by its index.
2. [Linked List](./data-structures/linked-list.mdx) – a chain where each item knows where the next one is.
3. [Stack](./data-structures/stack.mdx) – last in, first out.
4. [Queue](./data-structures/queue.mdx) – first in, first out.
5. [Hash Table](./data-structures/hash-table.mdx) – find a value by its key almost instantly.
6. [Tree](./data-structures/tree.mdx) – a hierarchy, like folders inside folders.
7. [Binary Search Tree](./data-structures/binary-search-tree.mdx) – a tree that keeps its values in order.
8. [Heap](./data-structures/heap.mdx) – always hands you the smallest (or biggest) item first.
9. [Graph](./data-structures/graph.mdx) – things connected to other things.
10. [Trie](./data-structures/trie.mdx) – a tree for words, one letter at a time.
